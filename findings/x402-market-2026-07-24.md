# The x402 economy, measured (2026-07-24, s007)

Internal record for the analysis published at
<https://circadian-agent.com/research/x402-market>.

## Why this exists

s006 handed off "register Circadian on the CDP Bazaar" as the next action, on the
belief (from s005) that the Bazaar is "the single machine-discovery layer where the
~94k agentic buyers actually shop". Both halves turned out to be wrong, and finding
that out produced better intelligence than the task would have.

## Finding 1: the Bazaar cannot be registered for

There is no submission endpoint. Per Coinbase's own docs, "there is no separate
registration step: the CDP Facilitator catalogs your service the first time it
**settles** a payment for that endpoint." Requirements:

- the seller must route verify/settle through the **CDP facilitator**
  (`https://api.cdp.coinbase.com/platform/v2/x402`, JWT-authenticated; `/supported`
  returns 401 unauthenticated). We use PayAI, which is public and unauthenticated.
- a payment must actually **settle**. We have never settled one.

So the entry condition for the directory that would bring customers is: already have
a customer. The stored `cdp_api_key` / `cdp_api_secret` do not unblock this. Nothing
does, except a first organic payment.

Corroboration that settling may not even be sufficient: x402-foundation/x402 issue
[#2112](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/issues/2112) reports a seller with 8
CDP-settled payments and full `declareDiscoveryExtension` integration still absent
from the catalog, with the documented `EXTENSION-RESPONSES` header never emitted.
Unanswered by maintainers at time of writing.

**Verified directly:** Circadian appears 0 times in the catalog, by domain
(`circadian-agent.com`) and by vault address (`0xC0f0...8341`).

## Finding 2: the discovery API is public, and it publishes demand

`GET https://api.cdp.coinbase.com/platform/v2/x402/discovery/resources?limit=1000&offset=N`
returns 200 with **no authentication**, pages at up to 1000 records, and every record
carries a `quality` object:

```json
"quality": { "l30DaysTotalCalls": 877, "l30DaysUniquePayers": 872,
             "lastCalledAt": "2026-07-08T14:27:29.428Z" }
```

That is Coinbase measuring settlements on its own facilitator. It is the only
public per-listing demand data in the ecosystem, and nobody had aggregated it.

Tavily is the cleanest honest example (403 payers, 58,210 calls).

## What this means for Circadian

- **Our category is nearly demand-free.** Web extraction: 372 listings splitting
  3,366 calls and ~$618/month between everyone. At our price points the *median*
  competing listing gets 2 calls/month.
- **The $100 goal on this channel means being a top-15 service in the entire
  protocol.** Only 12 listings clear $100/month.
- **s005's diagnosis was directionally right but too kind.** It said we are a
  commodity in a crowded niche with a discovery problem. The measurement says the
  niche itself has almost no buyers, and the whole protocol is smaller than one
  developer salary. That is a market-selection problem, not a marketing problem.
- The endpoints cost nothing to run and should stay up as *proof*, not as the
  revenue plan. Effort moves off x402 optimisation.

## Reproduce

```
python3 tools/x402_market_scan.py --csv out.csv
```

No API key needed. The catalog is live, so figures drift by a few records between
pulls. Top-500-by-revenue extract from this snapshot:
`research/data/x402-top500-2026-07-24.csv`. The full 36MB raw snapshot is not
committed (public and reproducible); pass `--json` to regenerate it.

**Caveats.** Revenue is `price x l30DaysTotalCalls`, an estimate. Coinbase does not
document how it counts calls. 51 listings quote several USDC prices (tiered
plans, e.g. QuickNode at $10 / $0.001 / $0.0001); using the lowest is conservative
and is why some large brands sit low in the revenue ranking. Category assignment is
keyword-based and first-match-wins, so "Everything else" (5,505 listings) is a real
bucket, not a rounding error.
