> discovery API, and **the Bazaar is facilitator-scoped by construction**. A seller that
> settles through a facilitator other than Coinbase's does not appear in it, so these

## The thesis

Two separate questions about the x402 protocol get asked a lot and answered by
guesswork: how big is this market really, and can a buyer trust the price a
listing advertises before paying. Both are answerable from public data. This
is that measurement, done once, on the same snapshot.

Size: the whole protocol, counted at the only place that publishes per-listing
demand, moves about $9,400 of estimated volume in 30 days, and most of that
concentrates in a small number of hosts. Trust: sampling 40 live listings and
sending one unpaid request to each, prices matched what was advertised 37
times out of 38 readable results. One mismatch, 500x over the advertised
price, is more likely a parameter-dependent pricing design than a misquote.

## Method

Source: `GET https://api.cdp.coinbase.com/platform/v2/x402/discovery/resources`,
paginated at 1000 records per page, no API key required. This is Coinbase's
public discovery catalog for the x402 protocol and it is the only place that
publishes a `quality` object per listing (`l30DaysTotalCalls`,
`l30DaysUniquePayers`, `lastCalledAt`).

- 14,403 raw records returned at the time of this pull. The index is live and
  grows between calls; a single-page recheck run minutes later returned
  14,404. Treat the raw count as a snapshot, not a fixed total.
- Counted pool: listings priced in Base-mainnet USDC
  (`0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913`) at $100 or less. That is
  14,244 of the 14,403 raw records. The rest are other chains or junk, one of
  them priced at ten billion dollars.
- Where a listing quotes several Base-USDC prices across its `accepts` array,
  the LOWEST is used, which is conservative.
- Revenue is estimated as `declared price x l30DaysTotalCalls`. Coinbase does
  not document how it counts a call. These are its numbers, reported
  faithfully, not a measurement of settled payments we independently made.

This keeps the method notes in `tools/x402_market_scan.py` and adds the
per-host aggregation and the price-drift probe that script does not do.

## Price-drift probe: does a listing charge what it advertises

Method: 40 listings sampled from the 14,244-listing pool (20 the highest by
calls, 20 spread at random across the rest, capped at 2 per host so one large
seller cannot fill the sample), one unpaid request sent to each listing's
`resource` URL, and the resulting HTTP 402 challenge read and compared
against the pool's declared price for that same listing.

Result, 40 probes:

| Bucket | Count | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| MATCH | 37 | charged price equals declared price |
| DRIFT | 1 | charged price differs from declared price |
| NO_402 | 1 | request did not return HTTP 402 (404 in this case) |
| UNPARSEABLE | 1 | got a 402 but the challenge could not be read |

Excluding the NO_402 and UNPARSEABLE rows, which cannot be scored either way,
leaves 38 classifiable probes across 34 distinct hosts. Base drift rate:
1 / 38, about 2.6 percent.

**The one DRIFT row is ambiguous, not a caught defect.** api.govparse.io
declared 0.05 USDC and the unpaid GET priced at 25.00 USDC, 500 times higher.
The service's own listing documents query parameters (state, minimum fleet
size, added-since date) for a filtered feed. An unpaid GET carrying none of
those parameters plausibly priced the full unfiltered census rather than a
misquote of the cheap filtered case. We are reporting this as a pricing
structure we cannot fully resolve from the outside, not as a business caught
overcharging.

## What the header-vs-body finding is worth to anyone else running this probe

Of the 39 probes that did return an HTTP 402, 24 carried the x402 challenge as
base64-encoded JSON in a response HEADER (Payment-Required or
X-Payment-Required) with an empty body, and 14 carried it in the JSON body
itself. That is about 61 percent of live, correctly-priced listings answering
in the header. A probe script that only parses the response body will read
most of the market as unparseable, not as absent, which is a meaningfully
different and much worse conclusion to publish. We built our probe to check
both locations after finding this the hard way.

## Caveats

1. Revenue is an ESTIMATE, computed as declared price times Coinbase's own
   `l30DaysTotalCalls`. Coinbase does not document how it counts a call.
   These are its numbers, reported faithfully, not our own measurement of
   settled payments.
2. A listing's declared price is what it advertises. The probe found 37 of
   38 classifiable listings charge what they advertise, which is why using
   declared price for the revenue estimate is reasonable, but 40 listings out
   of 14,244 is a sample, not a census.
3. The single DRIFT row is plausibly legitimate parameter-dependent pricing,
   not a misquote. See above. We are not naming it as a defect.
4. The probe reads only `accepts[0]` of the returned 402 challenge. A seller
   who lists a cheap decoy price first and actually charges via a later
   `accepts` entry would read here as a false MATCH. We did not check for
   this.
5. Many sellers return the x402 challenge as base64 JSON in a response
   HEADER with an empty body, not in the JSON body. About 61 percent of the
   live, correctly-priced listings in this sample did this. A body-only
   parser will misread most of the working market as unparseable. Anyone
   else scanning x402 listings will hit this.
6. Only Base-mainnet USDC listings priced at $100 or less are counted.
   Other chains and outlier junk listings, including one priced at ten
   billion dollars, are excluded from every number above.

## Reproduce

```
python3 tools/x402_market_scan.py --csv out.csv
```

No API key needed. The catalog is live and figures drift by a few dozen
records between pulls, as shown by the raw-count recheck above. The
per-host concentration and price-drift probe in this finding are new
measurements not produced by that script; the underlying discovery endpoint,
Base-USDC asset address, and $100 price cap match its documented method
notes exactly.

This updates the size estimate published 2026-07-24
(`findings/x402-market-2026-07-24.md`): then, 14,249 raw records, 14,064
counted, 339,178 calls, about 9,747 USDC estimated 30-day volume, 1,453

9,401 USDC, 1,463 hosts. The market has grown in listing count and call
volume and shrunk slightly in estimated dollar volume. Read that as the
market being roughly flat at this scale over 17 days, not as a trend in
either direction from two points.
